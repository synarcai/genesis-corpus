in a right triangle one angle is 90° and the two acute ones are 20° and 70°: 90 + 20 + 70 = 180, so 20 + 70 = 90. TWO ACUTE ANGLES TOGETHER MAKE A RIGHT ONE.
in a convex 4-gon the angles sum to 360°: (4 − 2) × 180 = 360. EVERY NEW SIDE ADDS EXACTLY 180°.
two angles of a triangle are 75° and 55°; the third is found by subtraction: 180 − 75 − 55 = 50. TWO ANGLES HOLD THE THIRD, AND THERE IS NOTHING TO MEASURE.

a triangle with two angles of 110° does not exist: 110 + 110 = 220, and -40° would be left for the third, while an angle of -40° is no angle. THE LIMIT HERE IS NOT A PROHIBITION BUT A CONSEQUENCE OF COUNTING.

one triangle has angles 45°, 45°, 90°, another has 20°, 80°, 80°. what do they share? the sum: 45 + 45 + 90 = 180 and 20 + 80 + 80 = 180.
one triangle has angles 75°, 55°, 50°, another has 15°, 75°, 90°. what do they share? the sum: 75 + 55 + 50 = 180 and 15 + 75 + 90 = 180.
