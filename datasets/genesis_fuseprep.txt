In Italian the preposition «a» and the article «le» ARE NOT WRITTEN APART: the two words fuse into one and give «alle case» (the houses). THIS IS NOT A NEW WORD BUT TWO OLD ONES GROWN TOGETHER.

«nel libro» — the definite article is fused with the preposition. But «in einem Haus» stands APART, because the article there is INDEFINITE: «*ineinem» is not written. NOT EVERY ARTICLE FUSES, ONLY THE DEFINITE ONE, and this holds in all four languages.
French fuses only four combinations: «de» with «le» gives «du livre» (the book). But «de cette maison» stays APART — «*decette» is not written. A LANGUAGE THAT FUSES DOES NOT THEREBY FUSE EVERYTHING.

In German «von dem Tisch» and «vom Tisch» are BOTH LEGAL (the table): the fusion here is the speaker's WILL, not a law. The Italian ban and the German freedom are therefore DIFFERENT LAWS, not different strictness of one law.
